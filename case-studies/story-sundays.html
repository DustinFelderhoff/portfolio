Story Sundays

An AI-powered family memory platform that helps families capture, share, and preserve the stories that matter before they're lost. It's also the product a multi-agent pipeline built, and the place where I decide how its AI behaves with the people who trust it.

The Problem

Family stories fade. Grandparents pass on and their voices are lost. Parents forget the small moments that shaped their children. Existing tools are either too public (social media), too impersonal (cloud storage), or too complex for the people who need them most. Story Sundays was built to be the missing middle: private, personal, and intentionally designed for intergenerational storytelling.

Core insight: People don't record family stories because they don't know what to ask. AI-generated prompts, personalized to each family member, solve the cold-start problem.

Key Features

🎥 Record · Multi-Media Capture

Multi-segment video recording, photo capture with Canvas compression (2048px, 80% JPEG), and file upload. MediaTypePicker for video/photo selection. AI follow-up questions in the viewfinder. Encrypted storage on Cloudflare R2.

🌳 Interactive Family Tree 2.0

3-generation lens with SVG connector lines, click-and-drag pan, add parents/children/siblings/spouses. Relationships table, invite RLS expansion, collaboration model. LCA-based relationship labels (Father, Brother-in-law, 2nd Cousin), divorce status.

🤖 AI Prompt Generator & Prompts Hub

Unified Prompts grid with 4-tab ViewSelector (Available, Assigned, Sent, Completed). 10 ENUM categories (family_history → wisdom). AI-generated personalized prompts per family member. NBA (Next Best Action) widget, prompt assignment system.

📖 Our Story · Family Origin Page

Per-archive family origin page that tells the story of each family unit. Auto-generated narrative from family data with photos. A permanent record of how each family came to be.

⚙️ Settings · Full Control Panel

Accordion-page with 6 sections: Profile, Notifications, Privacy, Archive, Members, Danger Zone. Auto-save on every change. Archive member management with invite resend/remove. Danger Zone for destructive actions (leave/delete archive).

📊 Smart Dashboard & Life Timeline

Time-of-day greeting, upcoming prompts with countdown, birthday tracker, Up Next context-aware widget, personal prompt inbox, Random Memory widget. Life Timeline with real data from stories table, infinite scroll pagination, loading skeleton, empty/error states.

🤖 AI Family Assistant

Phase 1 deployed: Cloudflare Worker + React panel + OpenRouter integration. AI-powered conversational assistant contextually aware of family members and stories. Extensible for future capabilities.

🔒 Privacy & Access Control

Row Level Security (RLS) on every table. Archive-based sharing. Invite-only membership. COPPA 13+ age gate. Time-locked stories (reveal on future date). Family Story Reactions with 5 curated reactions and Voice Reply.

The Multi-Agent Pipeline That Built It

Story Sundays is the product people meet. Underneath it sits a second system I designed and ran: a kanban-based pipeline of specialized AI agents that take a requirement and carry it through research, planning, review, implementation, verification, and documentation. An orchestrator reads the requirement and routes it. Each step is its own agent with its own fresh context, so no single agent sees the whole job from one angle.

The model mix behind those roles changed three times in a few months, and each change came from a pain I could measure, not from novelty.

  • I started with an eight-agent Claude Code CrewAI setup. It was powerful and it burned money. I canceled the Claude subscription in early July when the cost stopped earning the behavior.
  • I moved to GLM-5.2 plus DeepSeek Flash, one strong reasoning model paired with a cheap, deterministic worker. Better cost, but the thinking and the verifying still sat too close together.
  • Now DeepSeek Pro runs the reasoning roles, like architecture and planning, and DeepSeek Flash runs the mechanical ones, like verification and doc sync. They work as a five-role cycler.

Splitting them that hard is not style. It solves a specific failure. When one agent writes a feature and then tests that same feature, it can write a test that passes no matter what. It asserts what it wants to be true, mocks the seam it should really exercise, and goes green while the product is broken. I have watched that happen, logged it, and built the pipeline so it cannot.

The guardrails are mechanical. The implementer never sees how the tester will verify. For risky changes, a separate worker writes the tests first and locks them, then the tester checks the finished code against that lock with a byte-level diff. You cannot argue with a diff. I would rather spend a little more compute than trust an agent to grade its own homework.

How I Keep the AI Honest

The AI users actually meet in Story Sundays is real. It writes prompts personalized to each family member so a grandparent knows what to say, and an AI family assistant answers with context about who is in the family and what was already recorded. That is agent behavior, and I steer it with tests.

The product runs tests at four layers, from unit checks up to a real browser driving the full click-to-database flow. The rule on top is the one I apply everywhere: a green test suite is not proof a bug is fixed. Each layer may only claim what it can actually observe, and nothing is allowed to mock the seam it claims to verify. That doctrine is what kept about 1,900 lines of edge-case rules from regressing and kept twenty-plus database migrations leak-free.
